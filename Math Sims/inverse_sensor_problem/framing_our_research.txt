How to Frame Your Paper and Required Data
To get accepted into a top-tier robotics venue, your paper needs to tell a story of solving a massive hardware bottleneck with elegant, scalable engineering. Reviewers will be looking for specific experimental validations:

The Baseline Comparison (OptoSpine vs. FBG): You do not need to prove you are more accurate than a $10,000 FBG system. You need to prove you are "accurate enough" for macro-scale soft robotics. Collect data showing your sensor tracking a known curve alongside an FBG (or a highly accurate camera-based motion capture system like Vicon). Show that your 20 mm discrete nodes capture the overall macro-state with a fraction of the cost and processing power.

The Hysteresis Benchmark (The "Return to Zero" Test): This is your main attack on existing cheap sensors. Take a standard capacitive or resistive flex sensor and your OptoSpine. Bend them both to 90°, hold them there, and then snap them back to 0°. Record the data. You need to show that the flex sensor drifts and fails to read true zero, while your optoelectronic sensor snaps back perfectly.

The Kinematic Reconstruction (Math to 3D Shape): Reviewers want to see the translation from raw sensor data to a 3D model. Provide the data pipeline showing how you take 50 discrete 16-bit brightness readings, run them through your matrix geometry, and use a Cosserat Rod or Cubic Spline algorithm to render a smooth, continuous 3D curve on a screen in real-time.

Signal Integrity at Scale: Because your claim is a "cut-to-length" 1-meter spine, you must prove the electrical architecture holds up. Include an oscilloscope readout showing the I2C clock and data lines at node 1 versus node 50. Show that your TCA4311A buffers successfully keep the digital pulses square and prevent the bus capacitance from collapsing the signal.

Dynamic Response and Bandwidth: Soft robots can move quickly (e.g., whipping or rapid gripping). Collect data on your sampling rate. Show how many times per second your RP2040 can poll all 50 sensors and update the 3D shape, proving it has the bandwidth for real-time closed-loop control.
